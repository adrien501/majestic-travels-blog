Some people go to the Canary Islands for the sun. I went to Fuerteventura to learn how to surf. What I didn't expect was how much it would teach me about trust: in the ocean, in my body, in the unknown.

![Morro Jable town Fuerteventura](public/pics/fuerteventura/first-surf-lesson-fuerteventura/morro jable.jpg "Morro Jable, the quiet southern tip of Fuerteventura.")

## Why Fuerteventura? Why OTRO MODO?

Fuerteventura has wild beaches and year-round surf conditions. I chose Morro Jable, a quiet town on the southern tip, because I wanted space. No tourist madness, no clubs. Just ocean.

I chose [OTRO MODO Surf School](https://otro-modo-surfschool.com) because everyone said they were the best. I get it now. The instructors remember your name and laugh with you when you wipe out for the fifth time in five minutes. The whole operation felt genuinely human from the first email to the last salty goodbye.

## What surf camp is actually like

I booked five lessons over seven days, which left room for rest days. Times vary based on tide and wave conditions and they text you the night before. Hotel pickups or you meet at their office in Morro Jable. Most lessons happened at La Pared, a beach with consistent beginner-friendly waves. You get a wetsuit and a surfboard sized to your level. Each session ran about two and a half hours: theory on the sand first, then extensive time in the water. Supportive, not competitive. Even the wipeouts get cheers.

![Atlantic waves at La Pared beach Fuerteventura](public/pics/fuerteventura/first-surf-lesson-fuerteventura/beach and waves.jpg "Reading the waves at La Pared.")

## The basics of surfing

Four steps: get on the board, paddle (small paddles until the wave is close, then strong), glide, stand up. Back foot perpendicular to the board, front foot at 45 degrees, eyes forward, knees bent. Hands flat on the board edges to push up. Keep your feet slightly lifted while waiting for a wave and paddle a few extra strokes after the wave hits you to stay stable.

## Reading the ocean

Green waves are clean, no foam yet. Best to catch. "Left handle" means foam forming on the right, so you take the wave to the left, and vice versa. The current is sneaky: you'll drift further than you think and need to paddle back to your spot constantly.

## Safety rules worth knowing cold

Every time you fall, cover your head with your arms. Boards bounce. Waves push. A hit to the head is not how you want to learn this lesson. Never jump off your board feet-first as you can wreck your ankle. Fall backward or to the side, away from the board, never forward.

The hand signals: arms crossed means danger, fist up means I need help, one finger up means one last wave before leaving.

![Surf earplugs for water sports](public/pics/fuerteventura/first-surf-lesson-fuerteventura/earplugs.jpg "These became essential by day three.")

## The unglamorous part nobody writes about

Bruises happen, especially if you let the board drift into you during wipeouts. If your wetsuit doesn't cover your knees, ask for a longer one. And I got dizzy in the water. Turns out it was seasickness. Nobody mentions that in surf blogs. Motion sickness pills work but may make you sleepy. Ginger helps too, tea or capsules or candy. If it's not seasickness, it might be water in your ears. There are earplugs made specifically for water sports and they're worth it.

Surfing is demanding but also forgiving. You fall, you get up. Every single time.

> "Surfing is like life: every wave is different. Every condition changes. There will be highs and lows. You just try to adjust and enjoy the ride." — Andy, OTRO MODO Surf Instructor

## What surfing actually teaches you

By the end of the week I was paddling better, standing up more consistently. But the bigger thing was less technical. Surfing teaches you that control is mostly an illusion. That timing matters more than force. That falling is just part of the process. When you catch that one perfect wave and the board lifts and everything goes quiet for a second, you understand why people keep coming back to it.

![Hidden beach near Morro Jable Fuerteventura](public/pics/fuerteventura/first-surf-lesson-fuerteventura/hidden beach.jpg "One of the beaches you find when you stop planning and just walk.")

## Evenings at OTRO MODO

The day doesn't end at the shore. OTRO MODO runs pizza night on the beach during the week, with students and instructors in the sand watching the sunset, sharing stories. The kind of evening you don't photograph because it would be wrong somehow. Beach volleyball night is the other one: spontaneous teams, music, you'll feel welcomed even if you came alone.

![Beach volleyball at sunset Fuerteventura](public/pics/fuerteventura/first-surf-lesson-fuerteventura/volley.jpg "Beach volleyball evening at OTRO MODO.")

## Would I recommend OTRO MODO?

Yes, without any reservation. Whether you're a total beginner or looking to improve, they'll meet you where you are. Everyone speaks English, and some instructors speak Dutch, German, or French too. They're flexible and they genuinely love what they do.

## A few notes before you go

- Don't over-plan. Let the island set the pace.
- Trust your instructors. They know when the ocean is ready.
- Small wins count. Standing for even a second is a victory.
- Protect your head. Always.
- Look back to time your paddles. Look forward to steer.

I thought I came here just to surf. I ended up finding a version of myself I hadn't seen in a while. Freedom is real. Sometimes you find it a long way from home.
