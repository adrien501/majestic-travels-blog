I didn't come to Fuerteventura for parties, nightlife, or cheap drinks. I came because I was lost. Tired of everything. Tired of always thinking, always pleasing, always overanalyzing. I just wanted one thing: to breathe.

![Morro Jable sign Fuerteventura Canary Islands](public/pics/fuerteventura/fuerteventura-solo-travel/morro jable sign.jpg "Morro Jable, southern Fuerteventura.")

I stayed in Morro Jable, the southernmost town of Fuerteventura in the Canary Islands. Its quiet Atlantic beaches and coastal breeze are the whole point. It's not a place for shopping or clubs. There's barely any noise at all. You come here for the wind, the waves, and the version of yourself you buried somewhere between burnout and getting through the week.

I lodged at the [Tao Apartments](https://www.tao-apartments.com), a few minutes' walk from the beach: simple, clean, rooftop and a quiet pool. A big Eurospar right across the street for everything else. No luxury, no chaos. A base. For once I didn't have to plan every hour. I let the island set the pace.

Getting here was easy. I booked [Canary Shuttle](https://www.canaryshutle.com/en) four days in advance (reliable, recommended) and used the [Tiade line bus](https://tiadhe.com/) to reach Jandía. Affordable and straightforward.

## Surfing saved me

I booked five surf lessons with [OTRO MODO Surf School](https://otro-modo-surfschool.com). They were the best part of the trip. The instructors adjusted classes each day to find the right conditions. You don't know your lesson time until the night before, which felt strange at first and then felt like the whole point. No overthinking. Just wake up, check the message, show up.

Most days we went to La Pared, a rugged beach on the west coast where the waves are wild and raw. Theory on the sand first, then a long time in the water. The biggest thing I learned had nothing to do with technique. It was to let go. To stop fighting the current. To stop fighting myself.

## The honest part about struggling

One session I got dizzy mid-lesson. My body was swaying like I was in a car doing loops. Seasickness. Nobody talks about that in surf content. I got through it and found what worked:

- Ask for a longer wetsuit if you're a beginner. Your knees will thank you.
- Hold your board tight or you'll come back bruised.
- Ginger helps with dizziness. Seasick tablets from the pharmacy work too, though they may make you tired.
- If it's not seasickness, it might be water in your ears. There are earplugs made specifically for surfing.

Two and a half hours in the ocean will wreck your body. But in the best way. You come out wrecked, smiling, and proud.

## Island wandering and wild beaches

Beyond surfing, I let myself drift. Through Morro Jable with no expectations and no itinerary. Sunsets became rituals. Dawns became meditations. Stargazing from a towel at the edge of the beach with only waves beside me.

![Morro Jable beach at night Fuerteventura](public/pics/fuerteventura/fuerteventura-solo-travel/morro jable at night on the beach.webp "Morro Jable at night.")

One afternoon I explored the ruins of the 5-star resort Stella Canaris. Palm trees growing through crumbling halls. It felt like I was inside The Last of Us, a lone survivor wandering through a forgotten world. Creepy in the best way. Things fall apart and still stand. That stuck with me.

![Abandoned Stella Canaris resort ruins Fuerteventura](public/pics/fuerteventura/fuerteventura-solo-travel/stella_canaris.jpg "The ruins of Stella Canaris.")

## Hidden beaches worth finding

- Playa de las Coloradas: walk from Morro Jable past the harbor. The trail goes through mountains and cliffs. Once there it's just you.
- Playa de los Ojos: sea caves, only accessible at low tide, a bit west of town.
- Cofete Beach: one of the wildest, most remote beaches on the island. Bring a good car. It's hidden behind a mountain range and the drive alone is worth it.
- Jandía Beach and Lighthouse (Playa del Matorral): calm, endless sand. A beautiful lighthouse at the end.

## Local eats and evenings

- [La Farola del Mar](https://www.lafaroladelmar.net/): sea-view restaurant, local fish and traditional dishes.
- [San Borondón II](https://g.co/kgs/fz1aVg0): tapas bar, locals go here.
- [La Isla](https://g.co/kgs/WisdZsa): burgers, bocadillos, casual tapas.
- [The Docs Steak House](https://g.co/kgs/rWiB4qx): for meat.
- Cafetería Lemon Pie: cakes and a slow afternoon.

For late evenings: G-Skipper Bar has billiards, darts, and a rooftop DJ. Club Tortuga for dancing.

## Getting around on days off

- Car hire: [Do You Spain](https://www.doyouspain.com), ideal for the Cofete trip.
- Bikes: [Volcano Bike](https://www.volcano-bike.com/en/stations/volcano-bike-morro-jable) or [CCT Bike Rental](https://www.cctbikerental.com/rentals/jandia-bike-rentals/).
- Diving: [FuerteOceán](https://www.fuerteocean.com), beginner and advanced.
- Dolphin and whale watching: [Fuerteventura Full Experience](https://fuerteventurafullexperience.com/).
- Wildlife park: [Oasis Park Fuerteventura](https://mercaoasispark.com/el-mercado).

## What this trip actually was

I didn't go to Fuerteventura to escape life. I went there to remember it. To feel something again. To remind myself that silence is healing, that nature doesn't ask questions, and that maybe I'm not as broken as I'd been telling myself.

If you feel like the world is too loud and your thoughts are too heavy, go somewhere raw. Alone. Fuerteventura gave me space to be a beginner again. To fail. To breathe. To glide. That was enough.
