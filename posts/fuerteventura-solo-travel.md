I did not go to Fuerteventura because I was brave. I went because I was tired of hearing myself explain why I needed a break and then doing nothing with that information.

Morro Jable met me with wind first. Not postcard wind. Not soft, cinematic wind. The kind that gets under your shirt, rearranges your hair, and makes it impossible to pretend you are in control of the day.

## The first morning

The beach looked too wide for one person. That was the first thing I noticed. There was space everywhere, and after months of crowded thoughts, space felt almost suspicious.

I walked without headphones. That felt dramatic for about seven minutes, then necessary. The ocean made enough noise for both of us.

## The surf lesson

I was nervous before the surf lesson in a way adults rarely admit out loud. It was not just fear of falling. It was fear of being visibly new at something.

Then I fell. Immediately. Repeatedly. Almost efficiently.

> The relief of beginnerhood is that failure stops being theoretical.

By the end, I had stood up for maybe three seconds. It was not majestic. It was enough.

## What stayed

The trip did not fix my life. That would be too neat, and travel writing lies when it promises transformation on a schedule.

But Fuerteventura gave me a quieter nervous system for a few days. It reminded me that I could be alone without performing solitude. I could be bad at something and still enjoy the morning.

That is the part I brought home.
