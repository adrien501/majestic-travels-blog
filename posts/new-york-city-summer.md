New York in summer is not subtle. It sweats. It shouts. It turns every subway platform into a test of character and every late-night walk into a scene you are certain someone has filmed before.

And still, I love it most when it is too much.

## Start with one neighborhood

Do not try to conquer the city before lunch. Pick one neighborhood and let the day expand from there.

- Morning: coffee and a slow walk before the streets get loud
- Afternoon: museum, bookstore, park, or ferry
- Evening: rooftop, jazz bar, or a table outside

## The useful rule

Plan one anchor per day. Everything else can be discovered.

That rule keeps the city from turning into a checklist. You need enough structure to avoid decision fatigue and enough looseness to let New York interrupt you.

## What to remember

The best part of New York is rarely the thing you booked. It is the conversation you overhear, the street you take by mistake, the bakery you enter only because the rain starts hard.
