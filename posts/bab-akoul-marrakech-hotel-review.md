I didn't plan to write about this trip. It was supposed to be a quiet few days in Marrakech with my mom and my sister, staying at a place we'd heard about through a personal connection. But **Bab Akoul** surprised me enough that I thought, if someone out there is Googling this place, they deserve more than a booking page. So here's what actually happened.

We stayed at two of the three **Bab Akoul** properties over four nights in May: **Bab Akoul Atlas** and **Bab Akoul Palmeraie**. The third one, **Bab Akoul Medina**, wasn't operational yet.

## Bab Akoul Atlas: a villa to yourself

A driver was waiting for us at Marrakech airport. Real nice guy. After a 45-minute drive through dusty roads lined with palm trees and olive groves, we arrived at **Bab Akoul Atlas**. They welcomed us with Moroccan biscuits and mint tea, which was a nice touch.

The property was something else. We had an entire villa to ourselves. Private bedrooms, a living area, and this African-inspired decor that felt warm without trying too hard. Earthy tones, woven baskets on the walls, "BAB" stitched into the towels.

And then there was the pool. Tropical plants everywhere, bougainvillea, and the **Atlas Mountains** sitting right behind it all. I went for a swim at 9pm that first night. The whole place was lit up in green and gold, reflecting off the water. That became my thing for the rest of the trip: a 9pm dive, every night, sometimes in the dark, sometimes under the stars.

![Pool at Bab Akoul Atlas with the Atlas Mountains visible in the background](public/pics/marrakech/marrakech-pool-atlas-mountains.jpg)

Breakfast was always the same and always enough. Moroccan crepes, toast, confiture, honey, and an omelet if you asked. The kind of breakfast where you sit outside, hear nothing but birds, and realize you haven't checked your phone in hours.

Fair warning: the WiFi at Atlas is basically nonexistent. You'll get a weak signal near the lobby, and that's about it. For us, that was a feature, not a bug. But if you need to work, plan accordingly.

On our last day there, we walked outside the property to see the olive garden and the landscape around it. We tried to see the Atlas Mountains clearly but the 39-degree heat had everything wrapped in haze. The rooftop terrace still had a nice panorama though. Imagine that view in winter with snow on the peaks.

We also got a tour of a new wellness space they're building: showers, massage rooms, a hammam, a jacuzzi. Not operational yet, but the interior looked really good. Something to keep an eye on for future guests.

## Bab Akoul Palmeraie: the livelier sister

After two nights at Atlas, the same driver picked us up and brought us to **Bab Akoul Palmeraie**. I'll be honest, the first impression was a bit of a shock.

Where Atlas felt like our private retreat, Palmeraie was a proper resort. Ambient music playing, kids running around, people in both pools. The unheated pool was placed right in front of our room, which meant people sitting not far from our space all day. Different vibe entirely.

![Room at Bab Akoul Palmeraie with earthy decor and handwoven textiles](public/pics/marrakech/marrakech-villa-bedroom.jpg)

But Palmeraie had its own thing going. The staff was really friendly, noticeably more of them than at Atlas, and they all made a real effort. We sat in the main area outside the restaurant, ordered cold Colas and a plate of six mini spring rolls stuffed with beef, vegetables, and goat cheese. Those were good.

The lobby area in the evenings was a nice spot. Sofas, a fireplace, tables where you could sit and read or get some work done. One night, after my late swim, I parked myself on the sofa in front of the fire with my book. That's when the cat showed up.

A tiny white cat, skin and bones, half-blind. The staff told us he was 15 years old. We'd given him some chicken earlier and now he came back looking for warmth. He just sat next to me, quiet. My mom and sister eventually headed to the room (picking the last orange off the tree on their way out). I stayed with the cat for a while.

## The food

The **couscous** on our first night at Atlas was the best I've ever had. Not exaggerating. When we came back to Atlas on day four, we specifically asked for it again. Just as good the second time.

The rest of the food was a mixed bag. Breakfast was solid every day. Dinner was a set menu, and I'm not sure if other options existed.

The chicken dish was good. The mini spring rolls at Palmeraie were a nice find. But one night we got a cold bell pepper entree drenched in oil and spices that was basically inedible. The server gave us a look when we left it untouched. Another night, the main was three dry blocks of beef with no vegetables, no sauce, nothing. The almond sablee for dessert was fine but forgettable.

The kitchen can clearly cook (that couscous still haunts me), but the daily menu is a gamble. If you're staying multiple nights, ask for specific dishes. We did on day four and it worked.

## Golden hour and late-night dives

The thing I'll remember most is the ritual. Every evening the sun would drop behind the palm trees and the pool turned into this mirror of gold and silhouettes. I'd swim, watch the light shift, then shower and find a quiet corner somewhere.

![Sunset reflected in the still pool water at Bab Akoul, palm tree silhouettes lining the horizon](public/pics/marrakech/marrakech-sunset-pool-reflection.jpg)

At Atlas, the pool was ours alone. I swam three times in one day when it hit 39 degrees. At Palmeraie, I bounced between the two pools, though the warm one was almost too hot. More like a jacuzzi than a swimming pool.

The last evening was the best one. We said goodbye to Soufiane the server who kept coming over just to talk, to the rest of the staff, to everyone who made the place feel like more than a hotel. Paid the checks, walked back to our African villa, packed our bags, and set the alarm for 4:15am.

![Palm trees silhouetted against a golden Marrakech sunset](public/pics/marrakech/marrakech-sunset-palms.jpg)

A driver picked us up at five. Airport by 5:40, plane at 8:18, landed in Belgium at 11:45. And just like that, the quiet was over.

## Practical info

- [Bab Akoul](https://www.bab-akoul.com/) has three locations near Marrakech. Atlas is intimate, private villas surrounded by olive groves. Palmeraie is a larger resort with more of a social atmosphere. Medina is right in the center of Marrakech, good if you want to explore the souks and the city but still come back somewhere quiet to relax. Medina wasn't operational yet when we visited (May 2026)
- A driver picks you up at Marrakech airport. About 45 minutes to the properties
- Breakfast included daily. Moroccan crepes, bread, confiture, honey, and eggs
- Dinner is a set menu of the day. You can request specific dishes in advance
- Each property has at least one pool. Medina will have a heated pool
- WiFi was limited when we stayed (lobby only at Atlas, better at Palmeraie), but they're working on improving it
- Atlas if you want total privacy. Palmeraie if you like being around other guests. Medina for city explorers who still want a retreat
- Book: [Bab Akoul Atlas on Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/hotel/ma/dar-basma-marrakech.fr.html) · [Bab Akoul Palmeraie on Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/hotel/ma/bab-akoul.fr.html)
- My take: start at Palmeraie, finish at Atlas. Save the quiet for last

> If you've been to Bab Akoul or you're thinking about going, I'd like to hear how it went. The place is still pretty much under the radar, which is part of why I liked it.
