I didn't plan to write about this trip. It was supposed to be a quiet few days in Marrakech with my mom and my sister, staying at a place we'd heard about through a personal connection. But **Bab Akoul** surprised me enough that I thought: if someone out there is researching this place, they deserve a real account. Not a booking page. Not a stock photo. Just what actually happened.

We stayed at two of the three **Bab Akoul** properties over four nights in May: **Bab Akoul Atlas** and **Bab Akoul Palmeraie**. The third property, **Bab Akoul Medina**, wasn't operational yet. Here's the full, honest story.

## Bab Akoul Atlas — A Villa to Yourself

A driver was waiting for us at Marrakech airport. Real nice guy. After a 45-minute drive through dusty roads lined with palm trees and olive groves, we arrived at **Bab Akoul Atlas**. We were greeted with traditional Moroccan biscuits and mint tea — a small gesture that immediately set the tone.

The property was something else. We had an entire villa to ourselves. An actual private villa with bedrooms, a living area, and the kind of African-inspired decor that feels warm without trying too hard. Think earthy tones, woven textures, and details that whisper rather than shout.

![Villa bedroom at Bab Akoul Atlas with earthy decor and handwoven textiles](public/pics/marrakech/marrakech-villa-bedroom.jpg)

And then there was the pool. Surrounded by tropical plants, palm trees, and bougainvillea, framed by the **Atlas Mountains** in the distance. I went for a late swim at 9pm that first night and the whole place was lit up in green and gold, reflecting off the still water. That became my ritual — every single night I'd go for a 9pm dive, sometimes in the dark, sometimes under a sky full of stars.

![Pool at Bab Akoul Atlas with the Atlas Mountains visible in the background](public/pics/marrakech/marrakech-pool-atlas-mountains.jpg)

Breakfast was always the same and always enough: Moroccan crepes, toast, confiture, honey, and an omelet if you asked. Simple. The kind of breakfast where you sit outside, hear nothing but birds, and realize you haven't checked your phone in hours.

Fair warning: the WiFi at Atlas is basically nonexistent. You'll get a weak signal at the lobby area, and that's it. For us, that was a feature, not a bug. But if you need to work, plan accordingly.

On our last day at Atlas, we walked outside the domain to see the olive garden and the open landscape. We tried to spot the Atlas Mountains clearly, but the 39-degree heat had wrapped them in a thick haze. Still, the panorama from the rooftop terrace was gorgeous — imagine that same view in winter with snow on the peaks.

We also got a sneak peek at a new wellness space being built: showers, a waiting room, massage rooms, a hammam, and a jacuzzi. It wasn't operational yet, but the interior was beautiful. Something to look forward to for future guests.

## Bab Akoul Palmeraie — The Livelier Sister

After two nights at Atlas, the same driver picked us up and brought us to **Bab Akoul Palmeraie**. I'll be honest: the first impression was a bit of a shock.

Where Atlas felt like our private retreat, Palmeraie was a proper resort. Ambient music playing, kids running around, people in both pools. The unheated pool was placed right in front of our room, which meant people sitting close to our space throughout the day. It was a different vibe entirely.

But Palmeraie had its own charm. The staff was incredibly friendly — there were noticeably more of them than at Atlas, and they made a real effort. We sat in the main area just outside the restaurant, ordered cold Colas and a plate of six mini spring rolls stuffed with beef, vegetables, and goat cheese. They were genuinely tasty.

In the evenings, the lobby area became a nice spot. Sofas, a fireplace, tables where you could read or work. One night, after my late swim, I settled on the sofa in front of the fire with my book. That's when the cat showed up.

A tiny white cat, skin and bones, half-blind. The staff said he was 15 years old. He'd come looking for food — we'd given him some chicken earlier — and now he was just looking for warmth. He sat next to me, quiet, asking for nothing but presence. My mom and sister eventually headed back to the room, picking the last orange off the tree on their way. I stayed with the cat.

## The Food: Highs, Lows, and That Couscous

Let me start with the high point: the **Moroccan couscous** we had on our first night at Atlas. Best couscous I've ever eaten. I'm not exaggerating. When we came back to Atlas on day four, we specifically requested the same dish again. It was just as good the second time.

The rest of the food was a mixed bag. Breakfast was solid and consistent. Dinner was a set menu of the day — I'm not sure if other options were available.

Some hits: the chicken tagine-style dish was good. The mini spring rolls at Palmeraie were a nice surprise.

Some misses: a cold bell pepper entree that was drenched in oil and spices to the point of being inedible — the server looked at us a bit strangely when we left it untouched. Another night, the main course was essentially three dry blocks of beef with no vegetables or sauce. An almond sablee dessert that was fine but forgettable.

The honest takeaway: the kitchen is capable of great things (that couscous haunts me), but the daily menu can be hit or miss. If you're staying multiple nights, don't hesitate to request specific dishes.

## Golden Hour and Late-Night Dives

If there's one thing I'll carry from this trip, it's the ritual. Every evening, as the sun dropped behind the palm trees, the pool turned into a mirror of gold and silhouette. I'd swim, watch the light change, then shower and find a quiet corner.

![Sunset reflected in the still pool water at Bab Akoul, palm tree silhouettes lining the horizon](public/pics/marrakech/marrakech-sunset-pool-reflection.jpg)

At Atlas, the pool was ours alone. I swam three times in a single day when the temperature hit 39 degrees. At Palmeraie, I bounced between the two pools — though the heated one was almost too warm, like swimming in a jacuzzi.

The last evening was the best. We said goodbye to everyone — the staff, the servers who came just to chat, the people who made the place feel lived-in. We paid the checks, walked back to our African villa, packed our bags, and set our alarms for 4:15am.

![Palm trees silhouetted against a golden Marrakech sunset](public/pics/marrakech/marrakech-sunset-palms.jpg)

The next morning, a driver picked us up at five. Airport by 5:40, plane at 8:18, landed in Belgium at 11:45. And just like that, the quiet was over.

## Practical Info

- **Properties:** Bab Akoul has three locations near Marrakech — **Atlas** (intimate, private villas), **Palmeraie** (larger resort, social atmosphere), and **Medina** (not yet operational as of May 2026)
- **Airport transfer:** A driver picks you up at Marrakech airport. The ride is about 45 minutes
- **Shuttle:** Free shuttle between Bab Akoul properties
- **Breakfast:** Included daily — Moroccan crepes, bread, confiture, honey, and eggs
- **Dinner:** Set menu of the day. You can request specific dishes in advance
- **Pool:** Each property has at least one pool. Atlas also has a heated pool (very warm)
- **WiFi:** Weak at Atlas (lobby only). Better at Palmeraie
- **Best for:** Couples or families looking for a quiet Moroccan escape outside the city. Atlas is ideal if you want complete privacy. Palmeraie is better if you like being around other guests
- **My recommendation:** Start at Palmeraie, finish at Atlas. Save the quiet for last

> If you've stayed at Bab Akoul or are planning a visit, I'd love to hear about your experience. This place is still under the radar, and that's part of what makes it special.
