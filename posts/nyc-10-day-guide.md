I've lost count of how many times I've walked these streets, and yet New York City keeps finding ways to surprise me. This is everything I've learned from countless trips: hidden gems found wandering at dawn, the iconic sights done in a way that actually works, and the lessons I learned the hard way so you don't have to repeat them.

## Before you go: tipping

Tipping culture in New York is serious. Plan on 18 to 20 percent at restaurants, a dollar or two per drink at bars, and 15 to 20 percent for taxis. Hotel housekeeping gets a few dollars per night left on the pillow; luggage help gets $1 to $2 per bag. Have small bills on you. That $1 hot dog from a street cart will be one of the best meals you have, and the vendors appreciate exact change.

## The iconic sights, done right

### The Statue of Liberty and the Battery

Seeing Lady Liberty in person is a different thing from seeing it on a screen. Start at The Battery and catch the ferry there. The free Staten Island Ferry departs every 30 minutes, needs no tickets or reservations, and passes right by the Statue of Liberty. Outstanding views of the Manhattan skyline, the statue, and the harbor, and it costs nothing. Get there early in the morning if you want photos without a crowd.

### Times Square

I'll be honest: Times Square overwhelms me every single time. The lights, the crowds, the noise. Go at night when the billboards are fully lit and the whole square is pulsing. Yes, it's chaotic. That's the point. Grab a spot on the red steps, watch the people, and just be in it for a bit.

### Empire State Building and the best views

The Empire State Building's art deco lobby alone is worth the stop. The Vanderbilt Summit One Observatory has 360-degree views and glass elevators that are genuinely impressive. Rockefeller Center's Top of the Rock gives you the postcard shot with the Empire State Building in it, best at dusk.

> [Hotel Hendricks](https://www.hotelhendricksny.com/) has a rooftop bar where you can see the Empire State Building from an unusually close angle. Order a cocktail and enjoy the view without the tourist lines.

Westlight in Williamsburg offers a Brooklyn perspective of Manhattan at night with cocktails. Worth the trip across the bridge.

### Rockefeller Center

Give this a few hours. The golden Prometheus statue, the plaza, the ice skating rink in winter. St. Patrick's Cathedral is directly across the street: walking from the plaza into that neo-Gothic space feels like a different city entirely. The stained glass, the vaulted ceilings, the quiet. It's free. The Atlas statue a short walk away is another one worth stopping for.

### Wall Street and the Charging Bull

The Financial District has a different energy from the rest of the city. The Charging Bull draws big crowds. Get there early in the morning for photos without fifty other people in frame.

## Brooklyn

Manhattan gets the attention but Brooklyn is where the city makes more sense to me. The creative energy, the neighborhoods that actually feel like neighborhoods. Worth spending at least two full days here.

### Brooklyn Bridge and DUMBO

Walking the Brooklyn Bridge at sunset is one of those things that actually lives up to the reputation. Once you reach Brooklyn, head to Washington Street in DUMBO and find the shot of the Manhattan Bridge with the Empire State Building framed between the buildings. I've taken this photo a dozen times and still feel something each time. Jane's Carousel, a restored 1922 carousel in a glass pavilion on the waterfront, is an unexpected gem.

### Brooklyn Bridge Park and Pebble Beach

Walk the promenade, find a bench, and sit with it. The skyline changes constantly with the light. Pebble Beach inside the park gives you unobstructed views of the bridges and One World Trade Center. Go around sunset and get on the ferry as the city lights start coming up.

### Brooklyn Heights Promenade

An elevated walkway above the harbor with the financial district, the Brooklyn Bridge, and the Statue of Liberty all in view at once. Bring a coffee and take your time.

### Williamsburg and Domino Park

Domino Park is built on the former Domino Sugar Refinery site, five acres of waterfront incorporating industrial artifacts into the design. Williamsburg around it is good for vintage shops, street art, and coffee.

## Parks

### Central Park

I don't care how many times I visit; Central Park always does something to me. 843 acres and somehow you can still feel alone in it. Bethesda Terrace and Fountain, Bow Bridge at sunset, the Ramble when you want to feel genuinely lost in the middle of Manhattan, and Strawberry Fields. Rent a bike, bring a picnic, or just wander. Getting lost here on purpose is one of the better ways to spend an afternoon.

### Washington Square Park

Street performers, chess players, NYU students, musicians, artists, all of them converging under the Washington Arch on any given afternoon. The bohemian energy this neighborhood built over generations is still there. Sit on the fountain's edge and watch.

### Bryant Park

Tucked behind the New York Public Library, Bryant Park runs outdoor movie nights in summer and a winter village with ice skating and market stalls when it's cold. Surrounded by skyscrapers, always full of people taking a break. Good spot.

### The High Line

An elevated park built on a freight rail line, 30 feet above the streets of Chelsea. Wildflowers and art installations, the Hudson River on one side, the city on the other. Go in the late afternoon when the light is right, and walk slowly.

### Little Island

An artificial island park in the Hudson River on tulip-shaped pillars, with rolling hills, an amphitheater, and river views. One of my favorite spots in the city.

## Things you might not have on your list

### Roosevelt Island Tramway

For the price of a subway fare, a cable car above the East River with views over Manhattan. It's appeared in a lot of movies, and riding it still feels both strange and peaceful.

### Ghostbusters headquarters

Hook & Ladder Company 8 in Lower Manhattan. Still a working firehouse. You can't go inside but standing in front of it is genuinely satisfying if you know what it is.

### Chelsea Market

A former Nabisco factory turned food hall. Exposed brick and metal, lobster rolls, tacos, artisanal chocolate, constantly crowded. Worth it.

## Shopping

Macy's on 34th Street is enormous, historic, and has better prices than most tourist areas. Hudson Yards has luxury retail and the Edge observation deck with a glass floor at 1,100 feet. The Mills at Jersey Gardens in New Jersey, about 30 minutes out, has genuine outlet deals on designer brands if shopping is on the agenda.

## Broadway and food

Catch a show. The Lion King's staging is something to see. The TKTS booth in Times Square offers same-day half-price tickets, as does the one in Brooklyn. You may not get your first choice, but the savings are real. Black Iron Burger for an unpretentious, properly made burger. Street carts with long lines of locals: go. The hot dogs, the halal carts, the pretzels. That line is there for a reason.

## Day trips

Liberty State Park in New Jersey has Manhattan skyline views and the Empty Sky Memorial, dedicated to the New Jersey victims of 9/11. Two stainless steel walls frame the skyline with the Freedom Tower visible through the gap. Moving, and much less crowded than lower Manhattan. Coney Island is worth a quick visit for the Nathan's Famous hot dogs and the Wonder Wheel, which has been spinning since 1920, but I wouldn't prioritize it over other options.

## Practical notes

The subway is the fastest way around. Download Citymapper, it's better than Google Maps for NYC transit. You will walk more than you think. Comfortable shoes are not optional. Major attractions are least crowded early morning or late evening. Want Times Square to yourself? Go at 7 AM. Seriously. Public restrooms are scarce. Starbucks, department stores, and hotel lobbies are your friends. Don't wait until it's urgent.

## Ten days

The city will exhaust you, get under your skin, and probably overwhelm you at some point. Ten days is enough to cover the major sights and explore the neighborhoods and still have afternoons with nothing planned. Some of my best NYC memories happened when I abandoned the itinerary and just walked, following streets I didn't know. Hidden courtyards, unexpected art, conversations with strangers who became friends for an hour.

The city doesn't need you to be ready for it. Come anyway.
